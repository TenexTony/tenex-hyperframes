# 09 · Make it feel 3D

## What it makes

A revision with tilted panels, layered graphics, and soft shadows.

## What to attach

Open the existing project. Optional: a reference image or clip showing the depth you want.

## The prompt

```text
Revise the graphics in this project so they feel physical and have depth. Save a new version. Keep my footage, recorded audio, caption wording, total duration, and scene timing unchanged.

Look I want: [describe the look or name the attached reference]. Keep the current brand colors and readable type.

- Tilt cards and panels as if they were objects viewed at an angle. Do not turn every label into a spinning object.
- Put selected graphics in front of or behind one another. When they move, let nearer layers travel a little faster than distant layers.
- Add soft shadows. Darken a surface slightly as it turns away, so it feels lit rather than flat.
- Use true 3D only where it makes the idea clearer. Use layered flat graphics if true 3D slows the preview without improving the shot.
- Start and stop movements smoothly. Use a small drift only on graphics that benefit from it; stable charts and readable text can stay still.
- Keep text sharp when it needs to be read. A brief fade, rise, or blur on entrance is fine if it does not affect caption synchronization.

Remove stray frames, flashes, and broken overlaps at scene boundaries without trimming speech or changing the edit's length. Smooth awkward transitions within their existing time windows.

Before rendering, save six still frames across the video and inspect depth, text, and overlaps. Also play through every changed transition with sound; stills cannot prove motion is clean. Fix the problems you find.

Show me the revised preview and a short list of changes. After my approval, run the project checks and export an MP4.
```

## Tips

- Use this after the story and timing already work.
- Subtle perspective often looks better than constant rotation.
- Ask for pause removal separately; this prompt preserves your edit's timing.
