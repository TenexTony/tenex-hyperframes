# 15 · Podcast edit

## What it makes

A shorter podcast edit with titles, diagrams, and real footage for selected moments.

## What to attach

The recording and your target length. Optional: timed transcript, logo, brand guide, archival clips, and source links.

## The prompt

Paste the main prompt first. Use the two follow-ups below when reviewing its draft.

```text
Edit [your podcast recording filename] into a [target length]-minute [16:9 widescreen or 9:16 vertical] video for [your audience]. The source is about [source length] minutes. Focus on [main topic or purpose].

Keep each speaker's meaning, caveats, and natural delivery. Do not fabricate speech or use a cut that changes a claim. Keep the speakers visible for most of the episode. Preserve their names and roles: [names and roles].

First transcribe the full recording, or use the attached timed transcript. Check important names and numbers. Propose the sections to keep and cut, with source timestamps. Wait for my OK before changing the spoken edit.

After approval:
- Remove repeated points, false starts, and distracting dead time. Keep pauses and reactions that affect the meaning or mood. Do not shorten every breath.
- Make clean audio edits that do not cut through words. Avoid unnecessary transitions between ordinary cuts.
- Add titles at section changes and quotes or diagrams where they explain a point. Use [your brand colors] and [your font]. Keep text away from faces.
- Use real archival footage only where it clarifies a specific point. Start with my supplied clips and sources. If search is available, propose usable real footage with its source and usage terms before adding it.
- Do not substitute generated footage for requested archival footage. If footage is unavailable, keep the speaker visible and list the gap.
- Keep speech clear. Add music only if I supplied it or approved the track.

Save a cut list and an asset list in NOTES.md, including source timestamps, footage URLs, known usage terms, and where each asset appears. Work in sections if the episode is too long for one pass.

Show me the preview. After my approval, run the project checks and export an MP4.
```

### Follow-up: fix transitions

```text
Review every cut and transition in this project's draft. Fix stray frames, flashes, frozen images, abrupt audio changes, and graphics that outlast their scene. Keep the approved spoken edit and total duration unchanged.

Use simple cuts unless a transition helps explain a change of section. Watch each repaired boundary with sound, including two seconds before and after it. Show the revised preview and list the timestamps you changed before exporting a new MP4 after my approval.
```

### Follow-up: replace placeholders with real footage

```text
In this project's draft, replace the supporting footage at [timestamps] with real archival footage showing [specific subject, event, place, and period]. Keep the approved spoken edit and total duration unchanged.

Use my attached clips or source links first. If you can search, show actual footage candidates with source URLs, what they depict, and known usage terms. Wait for my selection before adding them. Do not call a generated clip, stock reenactment, or illustration archival footage.

If no usable footage is found, leave the speaker visible and record the missing shot in NOTES.md. Update the asset list with the chosen sources and exact placement. Show the revised preview before exporting a new MP4 after my approval.
```

## Tips

- Start with a short section to confirm the style before editing a full episode.
- Supply a target length; “make it tighter” does not say how much to cut.
- Footage needs a usable source and clear terms. Finding a clip online alone does not establish that you can reuse it.
