# Get a better first draft

## Give it more than one sentence

“Make a launch video” leaves most choices to the AI. Add your logo, colors, fonts, an example you like, and a few clear rules. Say what to avoid: tiny text, vague claims, flashy backgrounds, or transitions that hide the product.

## Plan before you open the app

Write down who the video is for, its one main point, how long it should be, and what viewers should do next. Then choose the prompt that fits. A clear brief gives the AI fewer gaps to fill.

## Point at a visual problem

Pause on the frame you want to fix. Select the element in Studio, then move, resize, or edit it. If your version offers a drawing or annotation tool, use it to point out the change. Otherwise, attach a marked screenshot or describe the element and time clearly.

For example: “At 8 seconds, move the headline above the speaker and make it larger.”

## Ask for real footage when you mean real footage

Say “Use real archival footage of [subject], with sources,” rather than just “add footage.” Ask it to list what it found and where it used it. If it cannot find footage, have it leave the speaker visible and list the missing shot.

## Try more reasoning for a complicated edit

If your chosen AI offers a reasoning setting, try a higher setting when the plan or timing is complicated. Compare the result. It can take longer, and it will not replace a clear brief or good assets.

## Bring back work you already like

Open the previous project or attach its reference frames and assets. Say what to reuse: fonts, colors, pacing, or the closing card. If Studio offers `@` project references, select the project from its menu instead of typing a guessed project name.

Drawing tools and `@` references vary by version. See the [source notes](../resources/sources.md#limits) for the scope of this guide.

## Keep text readable

Check the preview at roughly phone size. A viewer should have time to read every line. Put captions away from faces, key graphics, and the edges where social apps add buttons.

## Review the export

Watch the entire MP4 with sound. Check names, numbers, captions, cuts, and the ending. Still frames help with layout; they cannot prove a transition or an audio edit works.

Ask for one specific correction at a time. Save a new version before a large revision so you can compare it with the previous draft.
